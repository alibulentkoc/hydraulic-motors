Formulas and symbols

The few relationships that describe every hydraulic motor, in pure form and in the practical unit forms found in textbooks and datasheets.

The core formulas

QuantityPure form (consistent units)SI practical formUS practical form
Speed from flowN = Q·ηv / VDN [rpm] = 1000·Q [L/min]·ηv / VD [cm³]N [rpm] = 231·Q [gpm]·ηv / VD [in³]
Torque from pressureT = Δp·VD·ηm / 2πT [N·m] = Δp [bar]·VD [cm³]·ηm / 20πT [lb·in] = Δp [psi]·VD [in³]·ηm / 2π
Flow neededQ = VD·N / ηvQ [L/min] = VD [cm³]·N [rpm] / (1000·ηv)Q [gpm] = VD [in³]·N [rpm] / (231·ηv)
Hydraulic power inPin = Q·ΔpP [kW] = Q [L/min]·Δp [bar] / 600P [hp] = Q [gpm]·Δp [psi] / 1714
Shaft power outPout = T·ω = T·2πNP [kW] = T [N·m]·N [rpm] / 9549P [hp] = T [lb·in]·N [rpm] / 63 025
Overall efficiencyηo = ηv·ηm = Pout / PinTypical ηo: gear 0.75 to 0.85, vane 0.75 to 0.85, geroller 0.75 to 0.85, piston 0.85 to 0.95.
Torque from a forceT = F·rT [N·m] = F [N]·r [m]T [lb·in] = F [lb]·r [in]
Oscillator torqueT = p·V / θEnergy balance for any limited-rotation actuator: V is the oil used for a swing of θ radians.

Where the constants come from: 1 bar × 1 cm³ = 0.1 N·m of work, giving 20π; 1 US gallon = 231 in³; 1 hp = 33 000 ft·lbf/min, giving 1714 and 63 025; 60 000 / 2π = 9549. Typical efficiencies are indicative ranges only; use the datasheet for real selection.

Schematic symbols

In a motor symbol the triangles point inward (energy flows in as oil). In a pump symbol they point outward. Each triangle marks a direction the motor can be driven.

Symbols are simplified ISO 1219 style.
